# Creative Quality and Anti-Slop Review

Good output comes from an approved template, disciplined copy, real visual QA,
and a learning loop. A large model or a Canva connection does not provide taste
by itself.

## Creative brief that can produce a design

Before drafting, write:

- one business objective and one audience action;
- one audience segment and its current misconception/problem;
- one sentence the audience should remember;
- one proof mechanism: process, demonstration, source, testimonial, or result;
- one CTA and the destination/owner after the click or reply;
- platform, format, dimensions, page count, deadline, and approver;
- mandatory/forbidden claims, assets, and visual elements.

Do not use empty directions such as “professional, premium, modern.” Translate
them into observable choices: amount of whitespace, headline weight, image role,
card count, grid, contrast, motion, crop, and information density.

## Anti-slop route and evidence gate

Before a Canva write, create three to five route cards from a scoped source
packet and brief. Each route must use a different strategic or visual mechanism
(for example, field note, proof demo, myth correction, or decision aid), name
its audience tension and proof IDs, and specify exactly one distinctive move.
Changing only color, font, synonym, or decoration is not a new route. Record
the selected route and human decision before creating or mutating a Canva
design.

Carry one art direction per selected route. It should name the visual premise,
the one distinctive move, the rationale, and a semantic role for every
ornament. Keep copy, logos, icons, CTA, and essential layout as editable
elements. Bind the approved template/version, folder, Brand Controls snapshot,
locked elements, and editable slots to the same tenant/client/product/brand
scope.

The deterministic audit reports explainable findings such as
`generic_language`, `same_layout_cluster`, `decorative_filler`,
`ocr_mismatch`, `layout_collision`, `semantic_mismatch`, `wcag_contrast`, or
`rights_unresolved`. It stores five 0–5 slop dimensions and a 100-point
rubric; it never stores an AI-authorship probability. Final QA needs OCR exact
match, layout/overflow checks, semantic object/count/relation checks, WCAG
contrast and alt text, rights/provenance, recent-fingerprint similarity,
independent critique, and a scoped approval-package checksum.

Treat every visible word as a message unit, including a tiny eyebrow, footer,
page count, arrow, annotation, or caption fragment. Before Canva mutation,
record its distinct `information_job` or a justified functional role in the
`message_units`/`text_elements` manifest. Source, legal, accessibility,
navigation, action, label, branding, and annotation roles are valid only when
their rationale and provenance explain what the reader can learn, verify, or
do. A repeated theme header, decorative page count, standalone arrow, fake
annotation, or filler caption with no such job is the explainable
`REDUNDANT_DECORATIVE_MICROCOPY` finding. Remove it or give it a real job;
do not use a generic “footer” or “label” tag to preserve empty space. Repeated
CTAs and necessary metadata are false positives when their action or metadata
role is recorded and supported by independently validated provenance. Mutable
source-packet IDs are not approval evidence. Navigation/accessibility targets,
`label_for`, and brand-asset references must resolve to real content or
approved Brand Copy assets. An `action` role is reserved for an actual
CTA/action field with non-trivial action copy, and generic “Overview”, “Guide”,
or “Panduan” labels need role-specific evidence. An `information_job`
by itself does not excuse a page count, arrow, or repeated theme header: use a
justified functional role with a bound path and evidence when the element is
genuinely needed.

## Information architecture

For a carousel, use this default only when it fits the idea:

1. `cover`: concrete tension, question, or useful promise;
2. `context`: why the issue matters to this audience;
3. `explanation`: the clearest model, checklist, or process;
4. `proof`: evidence, example, limitation, or mistake to avoid;
5. `cta`: one next action.

Each slide has one job. The cover is legible as a thumbnail and contains no
paragraph. Put nuance in later slides or the caption. Use a diagram, screenshot,
or real object when it explains more than decorative stock imagery.

## Copy pattern

Caption structure:

```text
Hook: a specific question, tension, observation, or useful outcome
Context: what the audience commonly misses
Value: explanation, proof, checklist, or bounded recommendation
CTA: one natural next action
Discovery: only relevant terms/hashtags, never a keyword dump
```

The caption must add information that the artwork does not already contain.
Prefer specific nouns and verbs over generic claims such as “mudah, cepat, dan
terpercaya.” When those words are necessary, explain the mechanism that earns
them. Keep the brand's natural Indonesian rhythm; do not use corporate filler,
forced enthusiasm, repetitive rule-of-three phrases, or generic AI transitions.

## Visual system rules

- Use approved colors and fonts exactly; label missing values `cannot verify`.
- Establish a clear scale: headline, supporting point, detail, metadata.
- Use a grid and repeat spacing values. Align edges intentionally.
- Reserve strong color and outline for emphasis, not every element.
- Keep decorative elements subordinate and semantically relevant.
- Use one primary focal point per slide and enough negative space around it.
- Keep important text/logo inside the platform safe area.
- Use consistent image treatment, icon family, corner radius, and shadow logic.
- Do not rely on color alone; include text/icon/shape cues.
- For video, require readable captions/subtitles and a meaningful first frame.

## Rendered QA checklist

Inspect each actual rendered page at full size and phone-thumbnail size:

1. What is seen first, second, and third? Does that match the brief?
2. Is any text clipped, crowded, tiny, low-contrast, or visually fused?
3. Do line breaks create awkward fragments or change meaning?
4. Is the logo/footer readable without competing with the message?
5. Do image crop, lighting, perspective, and style belong together?
6. Is every ornament helping hierarchy or comprehension?
7. Are facts, dates, handles, URLs, and phone numbers exact?
8. Can the message be understood without color and with screen-reader alt text?
9. Does the caption complement rather than transcribe the slides?
10. Is there exactly one CTA and an owner for the resulting response/lead?

Describe observable defects and their locations. “Looks good” is not QA.

## Draft-profile analysis

Use this checklist when a draft profile is inferred from public examples or
user-supplied references. It is evidence for a draft profile, not an official
brand guideline. Keep observations separate from approved rules.

### Common strengths to verify

- A consistent identity anchor makes a post recognizable at thumbnail size.
- A clear headline scale makes service or education topics easy to scan.
- Repeated cards, checks, arrows, or other motifs can create recall when used
  with restraint.
- Educational hooks fit audience questions when each has a concrete mechanism.
- First-party proof is stronger than generic benefit adjectives when an owner
  verifies the exact claim and its expiry.

### Common risks to check

- Paragraphs and small supporting text become too dense on mobile.
- Multiple outlines, shadows, patterns, gradients, and photos compete instead
  of forming one hierarchy.
- Separate post types drift into unrelated template families.
- Captions and visuals use broad benefit language without one measurable CTA or
  an explicit proof mechanism.
- A feed overuses static posters and underuses process, demonstration, proof,
  FAQ, and interactive formats.

### Recommended evolution

Simplify the grammar of any approved visual system:

- one core grid, one spacing scale, and one reusable footer component;
- one primary headline treatment per template, not layered effects everywhere;
- a calm reading surface for explanatory content;
- at most one decorative motif family per slide;
- short cover copy followed by structured cards or steps;
- controlled template families for education, process, proof, offer, and
  corporate information.

Do not adopt exact colors or font families from an observation. Confirm them
with the brand owner or an approved source before treating them as normative.

## Common failure modes

- **Template wallpaper:** changing words while keeping the same hierarchy for
  every message. Choose a template family by information structure.
- **Visual thesaurus:** adding icons for every noun. Use one explanatory visual.
- **Caption echo:** copying slide text into the caption. Add context or proof.
- **Fake specificity:** inventing numbers, dates, certifications, or exact brand
  values. Mark unknown and block publication.
- **Design by adjective:** “make it modern” without constraints. Specify grid,
  density, hierarchy, imagery, and interaction.
- **Automated winner drift:** letting metrics rewrite brand rules. Use a bounded
  experiment and lead approval.
